## Vector Space

*Definition: Vector space is a special type of module where the scalars come from a field. In other words, a vector space is a module over a field.*
```lean
variable {K : Type*} [Field K] {V : Type*} [AddCommGroup V] [Module K V]
```
We see that :
- `AddCommMonoid` in `Module` is replaced by `AddCommGroup` in `VectorSpace`.
- `Semiring` in `Module` is replaced by `Field` in `VectorSpace`.

<br />

Note that `Field` is commutative ring with multiplicative inverses for all non-zero elements. In other words, a field is a commutative ring in which every non-zero element has a multiplicative inverse.

<br />

*Definition: A linear map between two vector spaces is a function that preserves vector addition and scalar multiplication.*
```lean
variable {W : Type*} [AddCommGroup W] [Module K W]
variable (φ : V →ₗ[K] W)
```
We see that :
- both `V` and `W` are vector spaces over the same field `K`.
- $\varphi$ is a linear map from `V` to `W`.
- `→ₗ[K]` means a linear map over the field `K`.

<br />

*Properties of linear map*

```lean
example (a : K) (v : V) : φ (a • v) = a • φ v :=
  map_smul φ a v

example (v w : V) : φ (v + w) = φ v + φ w :=
  map_add φ v w
```

<br />

*Definition: A linear equivalence between two vector spaces is a bijective linear map $f$ that has an inverse $f^{-1}$ which is also a linear map.*

```lean
noncomputable example (f : V →ₗ[K] W) (h : Function.Bijective f) : V ≃ₗ[K] W :=
  .ofBijective f h
```

<br />

*Definition of bi-projection map: A projection map is a linear map that takes a vector in a product space and returns one of its components.*

```lean
-- First projection map
example : V × W →ₗ[K] V := LinearMap.fst K V W

-- Second projection map
example : V × W →ₗ[K] W := LinearMap.snd K V W
```

<br />

*Definition of bi-product map: A product map is a linear map that takes two linear maps and returns a new linear map that maps a vector to the pair of its images under the two linear maps.*

```lean
-- Universal property of the product
example (φ : U →ₗ[K] V) (ψ : U →ₗ[K] W) : U →ₗ[K]  V × W := LinearMap.prod φ 

-- The product map does the expected thing, first component
example (φ : U →ₗ[K] V) (ψ : U →ₗ[K] W) : LinearMap.fst K V W ∘ₗ LinearMap.prod φ ψ = φ := rfl

-- The product map does the expected thing, second component
example (φ : U →ₗ[K] V) (ψ : U →ₗ[K] W) : LinearMap.snd K V W ∘ₗ LinearMap.prod φ ψ = ψ := rfl
```

That is , the product map takes two linear maps `φ` and `ψ` and returns a new linear map that maps a vector `u` to the pair of its images under the two linear maps:
$$
\varphi \bigotimes \psi : U \to V \bigotimes W, \quad u \mapsto (\varphi(u), \psi(u))
$$

Note that ` ∘ₗ ` is the composition operator of linear maps.

<br />

*Definition of bi-inclusion map: An inclusion map is a linear map that takes a vector from one vector space and returns it as a vector in a larger vector space that contains the original vector space as a subspace.*
```lean
``-- First inclusion map
example : V →ₗ[K] V × W := LinearMap.inl K V W

-- Second inclusion map
example : W →ₗ[K] V × W := LinearMap.inr K V `lean
```

<br />

*Defintion of bi-coproduct map: A coproduct map is a linear map that takes two linear maps and returns a new linear map that maps a pair of vectors to the sum of their images under the two linear maps.*

```lean
-- Universal property of the sum (aka coproduct)
example (φ : V →ₗ[K] U) (ψ : W →ₗ[K] U) : V × W →ₗ[K] U := φ.coprod ψ

-- The coproduct map does the expected thing, first component
example (φ : V →ₗ[K] U) (ψ : W →ₗ[K] U) : φ.coprod ψ ∘ₗ LinearMap.inl K V W = φ :=
  LinearMap.coprod_inl φ ψ

-- The coproduct map does the expected thing, second component
example (φ : V →ₗ[K] U) (ψ : W →ₗ[K] U) : φ.coprod ψ ∘ₗ LinearMap.inr K V W = ψ :=
  LinearMap.coprod_inr φ ψ

-- The coproduct map is defined in the expected way
example (φ : V →ₗ[K] U) (ψ : W →ₗ[K] U) (v : V) (w : W) :
    φ.coprod ψ (v, w) = φ v + ψ w :=
  rfl
```

Note that `φ.coprod ψ` maps a tuple `(v, w)` to the sum of `φ v` and `ψ w`:
$$
\varphi \bigoplus \psi : V \bigoplus W \to U, \quad (v, w) \mapsto \varphi(v) + \psi(w)
$$

<br />

*Definition of universal direct sum map: A direct sum map is a linear map that takes a family of linear maps and returns a new linear map that maps a vector in the direct sum of the vector spaces to the sum of its images under the family of linear maps.*

```lean
example (φ : Π i, (V i →ₗ[K] W)) : (⨁ i, V i) →ₗ[K] W :=
  DirectSum.toModule K ι W 
```
That is, the direct sum map takes a family of linear maps `φ` and returns a new linear map that maps a vector in the direct sum of the vector spaces `V i` to the sum of its images under the family of linear maps `φ(i)`:
$$
\bigoplus_{i} \varphi_i : \bigoplus_{i} V_i \to W, \quad \sum_i v_i \mapsto \sum_i \varphi(i)(v_i)
$$

<br />

*Definition of universal direct product map: A product map is a linear map that takes a family of linear maps and returns a new linear map that maps a vector in the vector space to the tuple of its images under the family of linear maps.*

```lean
example (φ : Π i, (W →ₗ[K] V i)) : W →ₗ[K] (Π i, V i) :=
  LinearMap.pi 
```

That is , the product map takes a family of linear maps `φ` and returns a new linear map that maps a vector in the vector space `W` to the tuple of its images under the family of linear maps `φ(i)`:
$$
\bigotimes_i \varphi_i : W \to \prod_{i} V_i, \quad w \mapsto (\varphi(i)(w))_i
$$

<br />

*Definition of universal projection map: A projection map is a linear map that takes a vector in the product space and returns one of its components.*

```lean
-- The projection maps from the product
example (i : ι) : (Π j, V j) →ₗ[K] V i := LinearMap.proj i

-- The inclusion maps into the sum
example (i : ι) : V i →ₗ[K] (⨁ i, V i) := DirectSum.lof K ι V i

-- The inclusion maps into the product
example (i : ι) : V i →ₗ[K] (Π i, V i) := LinearMap.single K V i
```

<br />

*Definition of universal direct sum equivalence: A direct sum equivalence is a linear equivalence that takes a vector in the direct sum of the vector spaces and returns the tuple of its components.*

```lean
example [Fintype ι] : (⨁ i, V i) ≃ₗ[K] (Π i, V i) :=
  linearEquivFunOnFintype K ι V
```

That is, the direct sum equivalence takes a vector in the direct sum of the vector spaces `V i` and returns the tuple of its components:
$$
\varphi : \bigoplus_{i} V_i \to \bigotimes_i V_i, \quad \sum_i v_i \mapsto (v_i)_i     
$$
